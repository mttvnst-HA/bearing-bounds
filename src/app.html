Method, limits and verification
  1. Lower bound (LB): finite element limit analysis (FELA) with linear stress triangles and statically admissible stress discontinuities on every edge, the exact Mohr-Coulomb yield condition, and a radial fan of elements at the footing edge. If the exact solve is not accepted, an inscribed linearised yield surface is used instead: an 18-sided polygon on Quick, a 32- or 64-sided lifted polygon (Ben-Tal and Nemirovski) on Standard and Fine. The stress field is extended to the infinite half-space beside and below the mesh (zero shear, geostatic vertical stress beside it, uniform horizontal stress below it, with yield checked in every deeper layer), so the result is a rigorous lower bound whatever the mesh size.
  2. Adaptive mesh: Standard and Fine solve on 2 or 3 meshes, each refined where the previous solution was plastic (the lower bound's plastic multipliers and the finite element upper bound's plastic strain), after OptumG2. The best bound of each kind is kept.
  3. Upper bound (UB): discontinuity layout optimisation (DLO), which finds the critical translational mechanism among every line joining a grid of nodes, on a domain grown until the mechanism is clear of its edges. A finite element UB also runs for layered profiles, slopes, combined loads and (Standard, Fine) frictional soil, or when you tick the option: 6-node quadratic velocity triangles on Standard and Fine, linear triangles with velocity discontinuities on Quick. The lowest valid UB is reported.
  4. Circular footings under a central vertical load on level ground are analysed as axisymmetric, with full three-dimensional Mohr-Coulomb (the hoop stress is a principal stress): quadratic r-weighted stresses for the LB, quadratic velocities with velocity jumps for the UB. These are rigorous bounds for the circle. Untick the option, or apply H, e or a slope, to use the plane-strain estimate with shape factors instead.
  5. The finite element bounds are solved first with the yield condition stated exactly, as second-order cones (SOC), by the Clarabel interior-point solver; its stress or velocity field is then checked against every equilibrium, boundary and exact Mohr-Coulomb condition before the bound is accepted. If that solve or check fails, the linearised problem (a linear program, LP) is solved with HiGHS instead. The discontinuity layout bound is always an LP solved with HiGHS. Everything runs in your browser in background workers, and a bound is accepted only from a feasible point. The solution detail says which solver produced each bound.
  6. The mesh follows every layer boundary, the water table and the slope, so no element straddles two materials.
  7. The analysis is in effective stress throughout, using buoyant unit weight below the water table for every layer. For undrained (φ = 0) layers this changes nothing but a uniform shift by the pore pressure. Reported pressures are gross total pressures at the footing base: the effective result plus the pore pressure there, so they compare directly with the applied load.
  8. Footing and loads: the footing is a rigid body that can settle, slide, rotate and lift off, on a no-tension interface that is smooth, or rough with an interface factor R (tan δ = R tan φ′, adhesion = R c′ or R sᵤ). Loads are increased in one of three ways: all together (V, H and M = V·e in proportion; FS on the whole load); V increased with H held (FS on V); or V held with H increased (sliding capacity, FS on H). When the load is vertical and central on level ground, a symmetric half section is solved.
  9. Factor of safety on strength (optional): c′, sᵤ and tan φ′ are divided by F with the loads held, as in OptumG2's strength reduction. The LB on the factor is the largest F proven safe by a lower-bound solve and the UB the smallest F proven unsafe by an upper-bound solve. "Slope alone" uses zero footing load. Below an undrained slope the LB is limited to 4 sᵤ / (γ × slope height) by the half-space extension, so that bracket can be wide.
  10. Soil models: Mohr-Coulomb with associated flow. A dilation angle ψ′ < φ′ uses Davis's reduced strength (an estimate of non-associated behaviour; the bounds are rigorous for the reduced soil). Undrained layers can be anisotropic (compression and extension strengths as ratios of the plane-strain sᵤ, Davis and Christian ellipse). Any layer can have a tension cut-off. Neither of the last two is available in the axisymmetric analysis.
  11. Slope: a slope on the right of the footing is described by the distance from the footing edge to the crest, its height and its angle, with level ground behind the crest and beyond the toe. The water table must be at or below the toe.
  12. Embedment is modelled as a surcharge equal to the overburden at the footing base, so the shear strength of soil above the base is ignored (conservative). The section views hatch that soil.
  13. Square, rectangular, mat and slab foundations, and circles analysed in plane strain, scale the plane-strain bounds by the ratio of classical bearing capacity with and without Vesić/De Beer shape factors for the soil at the base (using the effective width B − 2e). That adjusted pair is an engineering estimate, not a rigorous bound. H and e act across B only.
  14. The mean of the bounds is shown with ± half the gap as an estimate only; design on the lower bound. Self-weight bounds in cohesionless soil converge slowly (see verification), and there the upper bound is usually further from the true value.
